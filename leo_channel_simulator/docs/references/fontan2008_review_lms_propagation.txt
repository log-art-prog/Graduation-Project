SUMMARY
The land mobile satellite (LMS) propagation channel is frequently described using statistical models. These
models usually make different assumptions regarding the behavior of the direct signal, the diffuse
multipath component and the shadowing effects. This paper analyzes the theoretical formulation and
implementation of time-series synthesizers based on three typical statistical models: Loo, Corazza–Va-
talaro and Suzuki, describing their similarities and differences. The discussion is not limited to the
amplitude of the complex envelope but also to the phase variations and Doppler spectra. Finally,
guidelines are also provided for comparing model parameters supplied by different authors. Copyright r
2008 John Wiley & Sons, Ltd.

1. INTRODUCTION
Performance studies of candidate proposals for new land mobile satellite, LMS, systems for
broadcast and two-way communication applications rely on the use of synthetic time-series
describing the dynamics of the propagation channel. A number of statistical models exist that
try to reproduce the channel behavior for a number of propagation scenarios, e.g. open, tree-
shadowed, suburban, urban, etc. Models such as those of Loo [1], Corazza and Vatalaro (C&V)
[2] or Suzuki [3] may be considered typical examples that are often used for describing the

dynamics of the narrowband LMS channel both for geostationary orbit (GEO) and non-GEO
satellite-based systems. This paper deals with time-series generators based on those statistical

Two main elements are considered in narrowband channel modeling, namely, the direct signal
and diffuse multipath. Assumptions are made as to the statistical behavior of these two
components. Typical assumptions such as lognormal direct signal variations due to shadowing
plus a constant multipath power or constant direct signal plus multipath multiplied by
lognormal shadowing variations are quite common, and their associated complementary
cumulative distribution functions, CCDFs, have been shown to agree fairly well with
experimental data statistics [1,2,4].
One basic application of statistical models is generating time-series for dynamic system
performance evaluation. Statistical models, conversely to what happens for purely empirical
models, are very well suited for generating time-series. Statistical models make a number of
physical assumptions on the behavior of the channel’s main elements: the direct signal and the
(diffuse) multipath component. However, their parameters must be extracted from experimental
data. Thus, statistical models represent an intermediate stage between purely empirical models
and those of physical or deterministic nature.
In this paper, attention is paid to the narrowband channel case, which is the most probable
condition found in practice in the LMS channel [4]. Events in which the channel gives rise to
large time dispersion are seldom found, and correspond to spots along the mobile terminal route
clear of near obstacles so that far echoes with long excess delays can reach the receiver from

The term time-series is used here (narrowband case) to refer to the variations in the complex
envelope of the received signal, i.e. both amplitude and phase. When wideband conditions occur,
the term time-series would refer to the complex series for the different taps in a tapped delay-line
channel model. When a narrowband channel is simulated, the transmitted signal is multiplied by
the synthesized complex-envelope variations that encompass both the shadowing, fading and
Doppler phenomena.
Another feature of interest is the rate of change of the different channel components involved:
shadowing and multipath. Multipath fading is produced as a consequence of ray interference,
and gives rise to signal enhancements and cancellations taking place in a fraction of the
wavelength: a maximum of two peaks and two troughs can be observed within one wavelength.
On the other hand, shadowing is a slower process, which is directly related to the sizes of the
environmental features in the near vicinity of the terminal.
Furthermore, in the LMS channel, shadowing effects may show signiﬁcant changes in received
level due to large environmental features, e.g. received levels at a moving terminal alternating
line-of-sight (LOS) and blocked conditions. Typically, these pronounced, larger-scale variations
can be modeled into different ‘states’ (e.g. using Markov chains), i.e. the LOS and non-LOS
states. Sometimes, it is best to deﬁne the so-called GOOD and BAD states that refer to LOS or
light shadowing conditions, and moderate to heavy shadowing conditions, respectively.
On the other hand, the shadowing can also induce smaller-scale signal level changes around a
given mean value. For instance, this occurs when the terminal traverses a non-uniform tree-
shadowed area, i.e. different branch and leaf densities.

2. LMS STATISTICAL CHANNEL MODELS
In the cellular/terrestrial channel, multipath effects are best described by means of the Rayleigh
distribution, which assumes that the direct signal is totally blocked. However, for the LMS
channel, the most commonly used distribution is that of Rice, which assumes that the direct
signal may be attenuated but is not negligible. In any event, the Rice distribution encompasses
the Rayleigh distribution as a special case when the direct signal is equal to zero.
The Rice distribution is used due to the fact that LMS systems need to operate with small
fade margins and the direct signal must be present and unshadowed. However, recently, with the
on-going interest and the standardization activities underway in the ﬁeld of direct satellite to
mobile TV broadcast systems, Satellite Services for Portable devices (DVB-SSP) [8], it is
expected that larger fade margins than those of earlier systems (Globalstar, Iridium, etc.) will be
available. Thus, it is interesting to revisit the existing models and reﬁne their parameters,
especially for heavier shadowing conditions [5].
In spite of the expected fade margin increase, still, the direct signal must be either completely
unblocked or only moderately shadowed. Purely Rayleigh conditions, implying that the direct
signal is totally lost, are of little use unless the amount of diffuse multipath power is large
enough.
Normally, to make LMS communications or broadcast systems feasible, multi-satellite
diversity techniques will be required to achieve acceptable levels of availability. In broadcast
systems, terrestrial gap-ﬁllers could provide the required availability within urban areas,
whereas satellite diversity would be used in highways or suburban areas.

5. SUMMARY
In this paper, the assumptions in three statistical models (Loo [1], C&V [2] and Suzuki [3]) used
for generating narrowband complex-envelope time-series have been reviewed. In the literature
these models have been shown to provide appropriate ﬁts to measured amplitude data. It has
been shown that, in most cases, model parameters can be found to produce almost identical
cumulative distributions for different channel conditions, e.g. light shadowing, heavy
shadowing.
However, when synthetic time-series are produced using the various model assumptions, it
can be found that the amplitude series are locally different in that their character is either more
Rice-like or Rayleigh-like. These differences can also be found when analyzing the
corresponding phase series.
It is not the intention of the authors to indicate which is best, but only to point out their
similarities and differences. The authors have used in their earlier work the Loo model [1] as the
basis for their modeling work [4,5,19,21].
